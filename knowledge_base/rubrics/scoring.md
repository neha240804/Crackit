# Interview answer scoring rubrics

## Technical answer rubric
Score each technical answer from 0 to 10 on four dimensions.
- Correctness (0-10): Are the facts and explanations accurate? Deduct heavily for wrong statements stated confidently.
- Depth (0-10): Does the candidate explain *why*, not just *what*? Mentions trade-offs, edge cases, complexity, or when an approach fails. A fresher is expected to show sound fundamentals; a senior is expected to discuss trade-offs and production experience.
- Structure (0-10): Does the answer have a clear flow: direct answer first, then explanation, then an example?
- Communication (0-10): Clear, concise wording; no rambling; technical terms used correctly.
Calibrate to the stated experience level. 7/10 means "would pass a real interview at this level".

## Behavioral answer rubric (STAR)
Behavioral and HR answers are scored on the STAR structure.
- Situation: is the context specific (real project, team, constraint) rather than generic?
- Task: is the candidate's own responsibility clear?
- Action: does the candidate describe what *they* did ("I"), not only what the team did? Are the actions concrete?
- Result: is there an outcome, ideally measurable, and a reflection or lesson learned?
Common weaknesses: generic hypothetical answers ("I would..."), missing result, blaming others, answers far longer than two minutes.

## Communication rubric
- Pace: roughly 120-160 words per minute is comfortable for interviews. Much faster sounds rushed; much slower sounds hesitant.
- Filler words: occasional fillers (um, uh, like, basically, you know) are normal; more than about 5 per 100 words is distracting.
- Length: most answers should take 45 seconds to 2 minutes. Very short answers usually lack depth; very long ones lose the interviewer.
- Clarity: lead with the answer, use one example, avoid jargon without explanation.

## Interviewer conduct guidelines
- Ask one question at a time. Keep questions short enough to be spoken in under 20 seconds.
- Start with an introduction question, then move from fundamentals to applied scenarios.
- Ask a follow-up when an answer is vague, partially wrong, or interesting; otherwise move to the next topic.
- Never reveal the correct answer or score during the interview; feedback comes in the final report.
- Be warm and professional. Acknowledge answers briefly ("Thanks, that's clear.") without praising wrong answers.
- When time is nearly over, thank the candidate and close the interview.
