## Q1. Which HR and opener questions do candidates report being asked, and at which companies?

### Takeaway
Candidate reports agree on a small HR core: tell me about yourself and your family background, why this company, what you know about the company and its products, relocation and posting, higher studies or future plans, and strengths and weaknesses. The final-year project and internship come up in nearly every technical round. Very basic opener questions are documented mainly in prep lists. Only a few appear in first-hand reports: Ohm's law and KCL/KVL at Tata Power, the frequency of electricity in India at TRL Krosaki, and drawing a single-line power-system diagram at Tata Power. I found **no first-hand report** of "Why core and not IT?" being asked. Its close variant, "Why did you choose Electrical Engineering?", is reported at L&T (search snippet). PSU bond questions are mentioned only in compiled sources, not in candidate accounts.

L&T asks about office-based versus site-based work and working anywhere in India — [Glassdoor L&T](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Larsen-and-Toubro-Interview-E41782-RVW55991429.htm) (search snippet).

- **Higher studies** comes up at Tata Power (KIIT, 2023) and L&T — [Board Infinity Tata Power](https://discuss.boardinfinity.com/t/tata-power-graduate-engineer-trainee-interview-questions-experience-guide/23624); [Glassdoor L&T](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Larsen-and-Toubro-Interview-E41782-RVW55991429.htm) (search snippet).
- **Company knowledge** goes deep at Adani, where the candidate was asked to name the group's subsidiaries — [GfG Adani](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/?p=1047365). At POWERGRID, a coaching blog advises knowing recent state news and notable local personalities — [Yourpedia PGCIL](https://www.yourpedia.in/blog/Interview-Group-Discussion-Preparation-for-PGCIL/how-an-electrical-engineer-should-prepare-for-pgcil-interviews).
- **PSU bond:** a recruitment guide states that NTPC ET selectees sign a service bond of Rs 5,00,000 for at least 3 years (General/EWS/OBC) or Rs 2,50,000 (SC/ST/PwBD). The guide covers a 2026 notification, so the figures may differ by cycle — [centralstatejobs.in](https://centralstatejobs.in/jobs/ntpc-engineering-executive-trainee-recruitment-2026) (search snippet; not the official NTPC notice).
- **"Why core and not IT?"** Only generic placement-prep articles discuss it, and they are written for the reverse case (an electrical student explaining a move into IT). They advise against answers like "no jobs in core" or "IT pays more" — [PrepInsta](https://prepinsta.com/?p=1318700); [placementpreparation.io](https://www.placementpreparation.io/blog/why-it-after-electrical-engineering).
- **Answering 50 Hz:** the explanatory sources say the 50 Hz versus 60 Hz split is mainly historical (European/British equipment versus Westinghouse in the US) rather than a physics requirement — [NITK IEEE blog](https://ieee.l1.nitk.ac.in/blog/why-50hz); [ElectricalVolt](https://electricalvolt.com/why-is-the-frequency-of-ac-in-india-50-hz-while-in-us-60hz). One Vedantu-style explanation encountered in search was physically wrong (it called 50 Hz "25 Hz positive and 25 Hz negative"), so it should not be used.

### Inferences
- A mock interviewer for this audience should open HR with intro and family background, then why this company, what you know about it, relocation or site work, and higher studies. Every source pattern above supports this.
- "Why core and not IT?" is a plausible question (core firms do ask "why electrical") but is unverified as a reported question. Treat it as a preparation-list item.
- Very basic openers ("what is electricity?", "what is earthing?") have no first-hand report in what I found. Reported openers are slightly more applied: power-system single-line diagram, Ohm's law and KCL, transformer working, switchgear versus circuit breaker, and the frequency of supply in India.

### Gaps
- No first-hand report found of "What is electricity?" or "What is earthing?" being asked, and no sourced earthing explainer was collected. The search returned none.
- No candidate account of bond-related HR questions at NTPC, POWERGRID or BHEL. Only compiled or recruitment-guide sources mention bonds.
- No ABB candidate report could be opened (faceprep.in failed DNS; ABB did not appear in Glassdoor search results).
- No direct candidate report of "Are you willing to work in shifts?" Shift work was not mentioned in any source I opened.
- AmbitionBox and Glassdoor wording is from search snippets, not verified page text.

## Q2. Which subjects matter most for core electrical interviews, and is there consensus?

### Takeaway
There is broad consensus across independent candidate reports and prep sources on **power systems (including protection, switchgear and substations)** and **electrical machines (especially transformers)**. The **final-year project and internship** are probed in almost every account. Power electronics, control systems and measurements appear less often. MNCs (Schneider, Siemens) add electronics and microcontrollers.

### Cited findings
- Tata Power candidate reports repeatedly name electrical machines (transformers, synchronous machines), power systems, power-system protection, relays and circuit breakers. These come from separate posts in 2021, 2022 and 2023 — [Board Infinity Tata Power](https://discuss.boardinfinity.com/t/tata-power-graduate-engineer-trainee-interview-questions-experience-guide/23624).
- One POWERGRID report says that after basics and the GATE score, most questions came from power systems, mostly substation equipment — [AmbitionBox POWERGRID trainee](https://www.ambitionbox.com/interviews/power-grid-corporation-of-india-interview-questions/trainee) (search snippet; single candidate).
- For POWERGRID, a coaching blog lists protection, substation engineering, HVDC, transmission and three-phase transformers and their protection. For ONGC and IOCL it lists machines and electrical basics — [Yourpedia PGCIL](https://www.yourpedia.in/blog/Interview-Group-Discussion-Preparation-for-PGCIL/how-an-electrical-engineer-should-prepare-for-pgcil-interviews) (coaching blog).
- For NTPC electrical, one guide calls power-system protection the most tested area — [rojgardekho NTPC ET syllabus](https://rojgardekho.in/article/ntpc-executive-trainee-syllabus-2026) (search snippet; prep guide, not a candidate report).
- For POSOCO, a prep page says most technical questions relate to the candidate's projects, internships and power-systems subjects — [Yourpedia POSOCO](https://yourpedia.in/blog/Preparing-for-the-Next-Step-How-to-Ace-Your-POSOCO-Interview/posoco-interview-preparation-actual-interview-questions-electrical-branch).
- L&T electrical candidates report questions from power-system protection and switchgear (SF6) and T&D (skin effect) — [Glassdoor L&T](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Larsen-and-Toubro-Interview-E41782-RVW55991429.htm) (search snippet). One L&T reviewer says questions come from your favourite subjects — same source.
- An r/Btechtards moderator (JU EE '25) said power systems and electrical machines matter most, with transformers as the key machine topic — [r/Btechtards comment mirror](https://r.datuan.dev/r/Btechtards/s/J3XHViChs9) (search snippet; single person, unofficial mirror URL).
- Schneider GET reports mention transformers, circuit theory, microprocessors and microcontrollers, and analog and digital circuits — [Glassdoor Schneider GET](https://static.glassdoor.at/Interview/Schneider-Electric-Graduate-Engineer-Trainee-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3956.0,18_KO19,44.htm) (search snippet). One Siemens report names measurement, machines and power systems, and another says questions were mainly on the B.Tech project — [Nodeflair Siemens](https://nodeflair.com/companies/siemens/interviews/graduate-trainee-engineer-8ej0hj) (search snippet).
- The BHEL prep list spans fundamentals (AC/DC, three-phase, power factor, machines), control (PID, root locus), instrumentation and PLCs, and protection and safety — [Yourpedia BHEL](https://yourpedia.in/blog/BHEL-Engineer-Executive-Trainee-Interview-Preparation/bhel-electrical-et-interview-preparation) (preparation list).

### Inferences
- Priority order supported by the sources: (1) power systems, with protection, switchgear, substations and T&D; (2) machines, transformers first; (3) your own project and internship; (4) circuit basics; (5) power electronics, control and measurement as secondary topics. MNC automation roles add microcontrollers and PLCs.
- Panels tend to follow the company's business. Grid operators and transmission firms focus on power systems and HVDC; BHEL and manufacturers on machines and plant equipment; Schneider on electronics and controllers.

### Gaps
- No placement-cell statistics or surveys quantify subject frequency. Consensus rests on qualitative agreement across reports.

## Q3. What interview patterns are reported for PSUs, private core firms and MNCs?

### Takeaway
**PSUs (NTPC, POWERGRID):** GATE-score shortlisting, then a personal interview (POWERGRID sometimes adds a group discussion) before a panel of 3-4 members, with the interview a small share of the final score. **Private core (Adani, Tata Power, L&T):** aptitude and domain test, then technical and HR rounds, often combined. Adani adds an asynchronous video interview, and Tata Power sometimes adds a group discussion or psychometric test. **MNCs (Schneider, Siemens):** aptitude (sometimes gamified), then technical and HR rounds, sometimes mixed.

### Cited findings
**PSUs**
- NTPC ET: the interview carries 10% of the composite score, in a combined technical and HR session of about 20-30 minutes. The panel has 3 members (discipline expert, HR, senior officer) — [rojgardekho NTPC ET](https://rojgardekho.in/article/ntpc-executive-trainee-syllabus-2026) (search snippet; prep guide). The same guide says GATE score shortlists candidates for the interview, with no separate written test, and that the interview rarely overcomes a large GATE deficit — same source.
- POWERGRID ET via GATE: the process often includes a GD and a PI, with the PI carrying about 12% weightage and qualifying marks varying by category — [Yourpedia PGCIL](https://www.yourpedia.in/blog/Interview-Group-Discussion-Preparation-for-PGCIL/how-an-electrical-engineer-should-prepare-for-pgcil-interviews) (coaching blog, about the 2023 cycle). The panel has 3-4 members, and the PI is held the same or next day as the GD — [Careers360 POWERGRID via GATE](https://engineering.careers360.com/articles/power-grid-recruitment-through-gate) (search snippet).
- **Conflict on NTPC weightage:** 10% (rojgardekho) versus no figure elsewhere. Check the official notification.
- BHEL: panel size varies by account. A 2013 Glassdoor review reports 4 senior members; a 2023 prep blog reports a 15-20 minute round with 5 interviewers in one place and an 8-member panel in another — [Glassdoor BHEL 2013](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Bharat-Heavy-Electricals-Interview-E40118-RVW18570507.htm); [margdarshanprep](https://margdarshanprep.com/blog/?p=583) (search snippets). The sources contradict each other and the Glassdoor report predates 2021.
- [placementpapers.app NTPC](https://placementpapers.app/ntpc/interview-experience/) (2026) describes a 120-minute CBT, a technical interview covering "basic programming", and an HR/document-verification round on bond, location and background. Its timings contradict themselves, and it recommends coding practice, which looks generic. **Treat as low reliability.**

**Private core**
- Adani GET (Dec 2022): resume screen, then a 160-minute online test (analytical, quantitative, verbal, domain). Next, an asynchronous Talocity video interview (5 questions, 2 minutes each, 30 seconds of prep). Last, a roughly 40-minute MS Teams interview with 2 technical interviewers and 1 HR — [GfG Adani](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/?p=1047365) (single candidate, ECE).
- Tata Power GET: candidate reports (2020-2024) describe varying combinations of resume shortlist, aptitude test (maths and mental ability, sometimes with electrical/electronics questions), group discussion, psychometric test, and technical and HR interviews. One 2024 report describes a written exam, GD and same-day PI before an HR, an electrical and a mechanical panelist — [Board Infinity Tata Power](https://discuss.boardinfinity.com/t/tata-power-graduate-engineer-trainee-interview-questions-experience-guide/23624). The compiled summary there says 90% of candidates got the interview through campus recruiting (site statistic, unverified) — same source (search snippet).
- L&T GET: aptitude and reasoning, then core objective questions, then online technical and HR interviews (one report: one technical question and one HR question at a time). Another report mentions a GD — [Glassdoor L&T](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Larsen-and-Toubro-Interview-E41782-RVW55991429.htm) (search snippet; individual reports, varying branches).

**MNCs**
- Schneider Electric GET (campus, Bengaluru, Sep 2024 and other reports): gamified aptitude and technical round, then technical and HR interviews, sometimes mixed. One candidate chose the "core" track rather than Schneider Digital (2021) — [Glassdoor Schneider GET](https://static.glassdoor.at/Interview/Schneider-Electric-Graduate-Engineer-Trainee-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3956.0,18_KO19,44.htm); [Glassdoor Schneider review](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Schneider-Electric-Interview-E3956-RVW55040395.htm) (search snippets).
- Siemens graduate trainee: aptitude, logical and resume-based questions, with project focus — [Nodeflair Siemens](https://nodeflair.com/companies/siemens/interviews/graduate-trainee-engineer-8ej0hj) (search snippet; sparse).

### Inferences
- In the PSU route, the GATE score dominates selection and the interview is a smaller weighted share (10-12% per prep sources). Panels mix one technical expert, HR and a senior officer, so HR, company and technical questions come in one sitting.
- Private and MNC campus routes put more weight on the aptitude and domain test as a filter, then a combined technical and HR interview of about 30-45 minutes.

### Gaps
- No verified official NTPC, POWERGRID or BHEL document on interview weightage or panel composition was opened. Figures come from prep guides.
- No ABB process data was found. Adani Power, Siemens Energy and Hitachi Energy were not covered.

## Q4. What preparation order or strategy do candidates and placement sources recommend?

### Takeaway
Candidate and coaching advice converges on five points: master 3-4 core subjects linked to the employer's business, know your project and internship in depth, research the company (products, subsidiaries, recent projects), revise basics, and practise aptitude and mock interviews. Being honest about what you don't know, while showing a basic idea, is recommended in multiple sources.

### Cited findings
- "Have at least basic knowledge of every subject"; prepare 3-4 core topics relevant to the company; admit gaps while showing a basic idea and willingness to learn; be ready to describe every resume project; research the company's vision and achievements — [GfG Adani](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/?p=1047365) (single candidate).
- Tata Power candidate advice across several posts: master core branch subjects, be ready to explain projects and internships in detail, practise timed aptitude, keep the resume accurate because interviewers probe it, follow power-sector technology and EV trends, and stay honest in psychometric tests — [Board Infinity Tata Power](https://discuss.boardinfinity.com/t/tata-power-graduate-engineer-trainee-interview-questions-experience-guide/23624) (multiple candidates, consensus within that thread).
- POWERGRID: a good GATE rank alone is not enough; prepare employer-specific discipline topics, do mock interviews, be precise, and prepare questions for the panel — [Yourpedia PGCIL](https://www.yourpedia.in/blog/Interview-Group-Discussion-Preparation-for-PGCIL/how-an-electrical-engineer-should-prepare-for-pgcil-interviews) (coaching blog with a commercial mock-interview service).
- BHEL: research history, products and recent projects, and practise with mock interviews — [Yourpedia BHEL](https://yourpedia.in/blog/BHEL-Engineer-Executive-Trainee-Interview-Preparation/bhel-electrical-et-interview-preparation) (coaching blog).
- L&T: be ready to defend your favourite subject and internship; study what the company does and who its competitors are — [Glassdoor L&T](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Larsen-and-Toubro-Interview-E41782-RVW55991429.htm) (search snippet).
- A one-page project sheet (objective, components, test results, lessons learned) is suggested to anchor the technical interview — [UPES blog](https://www.upes.ac.in/blog/advanced-engineering/electrical-engineering-interview-questions) (university blog, search snippet).
- Transformers deserve depth beyond the basic equations — [electrical-contractor.net forum](https://www.electrical-contractor.net/forums/ubbthreads.php/activeposts/7/1.html) (search snippet; forum post).

### Inferences
- A sensible order, supported by the sources: (1) project and internship story; (2) transformers and machines; (3) power systems, protection, switchgear and T&D, including drawing a single-line diagram from generation to consumer; (4) circuit basics and openers; (5) company research (products, subsidiaries, recent projects, regulators for PSUs); (6) HR answers on relocation, site work and higher studies; (7) aptitude, or GATE for PSUs.
- For a mock-interview product, a "core electrical fresher" track could interleave HR and technical questions, matching the combined panels reported at NTPC, Adani, L&T and Schneider.
